## IRS Oversight of Third-Party Tax Providers

- The meeting focused on IRS oversight of third-party tax return providers, including paid preparers, electronic return originators (EROs), online providers, and tax software providers.
- The discussion emphasized cybersecurity risks, identity theft refund fraud, and protection of taxpayer information held by third parties.
- It was noted that multiple IRS offices have related responsibilities, but no single office provides centralized leadership or formal governance for all third-party information security oversight.

## Security Requirements and Standards

- IRS Authorized e-file Provider requirements reference the FTC Safeguards Rule, but IRS publications do not fully specify the elements of a required information security program.
- Publications 3112 and 1345 mention the Safeguards Rule, while Publications 4557 and 4600 provide additional guidance that is not mandatory for all providers.
- The transcript stated that minimum information security standards are not established consistently across all provider types.
- The six security, privacy, and business standards for online providers in Publication 1345 have not been substantially updated since 2010.
- The transcript noted outdated encryption references in the guidance, including SSL 3.0 and TLS 1.0.

## Monitoring and Oversight Practices

- IRS monitoring visits are primarily focused on EROs and emphasize physical security more than cybersecurity.
- The Internal Revenue Manual provides limited direction for monitoring electronic security controls.
- IRS revenue agents were described as lacking technical expertise to fully assess cybersecurity controls.
- For online providers, IRS can remotely verify only some standards and relies on self-certification for others.
- The transcript stated that IRS has limited assurance that third-party providers are adequately protecting taxpayer information.

## Outreach and Education

- IRS and the Security Summit have used outreach efforts to educate tax professionals about information security risks.
- Outreach channels mentioned included Tax Security 101, national tax forums, webinars, Stakeholder Liaison events, YouTube, and Twitter.
- IRS officials stated they have participated in more than 1,000 events since June 2015 where data security was a primary or featured topic.
- A challenge was identified in reaching paid preparers who are not connected to industry groups or who do not regularly use IRS communications.

## Security Incident Reporting and Data Use

- Security incidents are tracked through multiple IRS offices and stored in the RICS Incident Management Database.
- Stakeholder Liaison uses incident data for trend analysis and management reports.
- RICS uses incident data for risk-based responses, including placing TINs on Dynamic Selection Lists.
- Criminal Investigation uses incident information to support fraud investigations.
- Reporting requirements are not consistent across provider types, and online providers have more formal reporting obligations than paid preparers and EROs.
- The transcript stated that IRS does not have a fully documented agency-wide process for intake, storage, routing, and sharing of incident data.

## Governance, Authority, and Risk Assessment

- The transcript stated that IRS has not recently reassessed security risks across all third-party provider types.
- Existing monitoring practices were described as based on older assumptions that EROs present the greatest fraud risk.
- IRS officials said they do not believe they have explicit statutory authority to require minimum security standards for paid preparers and Authorized e-file Providers.
- GAO argued that IRS could still improve standards and monitoring within existing authority.
- The transcript noted that explicit congressional authority would help IRS establish and enforce broader security requirements.

## GAO Findings and IRS Response

- GAO recommended centralized leadership or governance for third-party taxpayer information security.
- GAO recommended incorporating FTC Safeguards Rule elements into Authorized e-file Provider requirements.
- GAO recommended requiring tax software providers to follow a subset of NIST SP 800-53 controls.
- GAO recommended updating ERO monitoring, conducting a risk assessment, standardizing incident reporting, and documenting incident data handling.
- IRS agreed with recommendations to regularly review and update security requirements, standardize incident reporting, and document incident data handling.
- IRS disagreed with several recommendations, generally citing lack of statutory authority.

## Action Items

- IRS: Regularly review and update security requirements for tax software providers and other Authorized e-file Providers.
- IRS: Standardize incident reporting requirements for all Authorized e-file Providers.
- IRS: Document the intake, storage, and sharing of security incident data across IRS offices.
- IRS: Consider updates to monitoring programs, governance, and security standards as discussed in the GAO recommendations.
- Congress: Consider granting IRS explicit authority to establish security requirements for the information systems of paid preparers and Authorized e-file Providers.
