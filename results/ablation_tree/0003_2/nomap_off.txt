## Climate Change and Extreme Weather Risks

- The transcript discusses the need for the U.S. Department of Defense (DOD) to more systematically incorporate extreme weather and climate change risks into military facility planning and engineering design.
- Climate change is described as increasing the frequency and severity of floods, droughts, storms, and sea level rise, creating risks to life, property, supply chains, insurance, and national security.
- Decision-makers need three types of information: observed climate data, information on past impacts and vulnerabilities, and future climate projections.
- This information must be tailored to specific decision contexts such as bridge design, county planning, and defense facility management.

## Current DOD Practices and Gaps

- DOD already collects climate-related information from sources such as satellites, weather stations, model forecasts, and sea level rise databases.
- GAO found that many military installations do not consistently incorporate climate risks into master plans.
- Many installations do not sufficiently assess the likelihood and severity of risks, or identify response measures.
- Climate projections are rarely used in facility design in a consistent way.
- Existing practices at some installations include sea level rise studies at Langley AFB, improved stormwater drainage at Fort Irwin, flood walls at Norfolk Naval Shipyard, and coastal erosion considerations at Camp Pendleton.
- Overall implementation remains inconsistent across installations.

## Facility Design Standards and Planning Issues

- DOD’s Unified Facilities Criteria (UFC) mentions climate, flood, wind, drought, and wildfire considerations.
- However, the UFC does not clearly require installations to conduct risk assessments.
- It also does not provide clear guidance on which climate projection sources to use.
- The standards do not explain how to incorporate multiple future scenarios into design decisions.
- As a result, planning and engineering design often still rely on historical conditions rather than future risk conditions.

## GAO Conclusions and Recommendations

- GAO concluded that without integrating climate projections and risk assessments into planning and design, DOD may continue making investment and construction decisions that underestimate future risks.
- This could lead to greater facility damage, increased repair and recovery costs, higher budget risk, and impacts on mission capability.
- GAO made 8 recommendations, including updating master plan requirements, issuing guidance on climate projection sources and scenario use, and revising facility design standards to include climate projections.
- DOD agreed with all 8 recommendations.
- GAO stated that more complete guidance and standards are still needed to ensure effective use of climate information across installations.

## Action Items

- U.S. Department of Defense (DOD): Update master plan requirements to include installation-specific climate and extreme weather risk assessments and response measures.
- U.S. Department of Defense (DOD): Provide guidance on climate projection sources, multiple future scenarios, and relevant time scales.
- U.S. Department of Defense (DOD): Revise facility design standards to require the use of climate projections in engineering design.
