## Climate Change Risks and Climate Information

- Climate change poses serious risks to physical and ecological systems, and extreme weather events are expected to become more frequent and intense.
- Extreme weather events can affect loss of life, food and energy prices, disaster relief and insurance costs, property values, and national security.
- Decision makers need climate information that is tailored to specific locations and uses, including observed conditions, vulnerabilities, and local projections.
- Federal agencies, including DOD, use observational records, climate models, and decision-support tools such as sea level rise databases and national assessments.

## DOD Planning and Design Requirements

- DOD requires military installations to develop master plans covering at least 10 years and updated every 5 years.
- Master plans are to be based on strategic assessments of mission and expected installation use.
- DOD facilities projects must follow Unified Facilities Criteria (UFC), which provide standards for planning, design, construction, maintenance, and operations.
- UFC guidance includes climate-related considerations such as floodplain development, wind, drought, wildfire, drainage, roofing, energy use, and erosion.

## Installation Planning Practices and Climate Integration

- Of the 23 installations reviewed, 15 had integrated some extreme weather or climate change considerations into planning documents.
- Examples of integration included:
  - Langley Air Force Base partnering with the City of Hampton on sea level rise planning.
  - Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson developing site-specific predictive models for coastal erosion.
  - Fort Irwin developing a stormwater drainage plan after flash flooding.
  - Marine Corps Recruit Depot Parris Island planning a sea level rise study for master plan updates.
  - Naval Station Norfolk and Naval Base San Diego incorporating sea level rise concerns into planning.
  - Camp Lejeune using study results to inform flood zone mapping and building siting decisions.
- Eight installations had not integrated such considerations into master plans or related planning documents.
- Examples of installations not fully integrating climate considerations included Joint Base Pearl Harbor Hickam, Pearl Harbor Naval Shipyard, Fort Wainwright, and Camp Pendleton.

## Risk Assessment and Use of Climate Projections

- Most installations did not fully assess risks from extreme weather or climate change effects in terms of likelihood, impact, and response options.
- Only two installations in the sample fully assessed weather and climate risks and developed response plans.
- Some installations used climate projections, but many officials said they needed more DOD guidance on which projections to use and how to apply them.
- DOD updated UFC master planning language in November 2018 to require consideration of climate projections from reliable and authorized sources.
- However, the report states that DOD had not provided detailed guidance on how to use projections, including time horizons, multiple scenarios, or specific projection sources.

## Facilities Project Design and Resilience Measures

- Eleven of the 23 installations reviewed had designed or implemented one or more projects to increase resilience to extreme weather or climate effects.
- Examples included:
  - Joint Base Langley-Eustis designing a hangar with an elevated foundation.
  - Norfolk Naval Shipyard building a floodwall to protect dry docks.
  - Camp Pendleton protecting coastline near a new aircraft landing zone.
  - Fort Shafter constructing flood mitigation structures for maintenance facilities.
- Despite these examples, most installations did not consider climate projections in facilities project design.
- The report identified only one project whose design was informed by climate projections: a Naval Base San Diego pier that was raised by one foot based on sea level rise expectations.

## Budgetary and Operational Risks

- Extreme weather and climate effects can damage infrastructure and create significant repair and budgetary risks for DOD.
- Examples cited include Hurricane Michael at Tyndall Air Force Base and Hurricane Florence damage at Camp Lejeune and nearby Marine Corps air stations.
- The report notes that costs from such events are often not clearly represented in the federal budget process.

## Prior GAO Findings and DOD Response

- GAO previously recommended that DOD clarify planning actions and update design standards to reflect climate change effects and projections.
- The report states that DOD had not fully implemented prior recommendations as of January 2019.
- DOD concurred in prior responses that tracking impacts and costs matters, but did not implement the recommendation to systematically track extreme-weather costs at installations.
- DOD has made some progress, including a sea level rise database for DOD sites, but has not established a defined process for incorporating additional climate projections into guidance.

## Conclusions

- DOD installations have taken some positive steps to address climate risks, but integration is inconsistent.
- Many installations still do not fully assess climate risks or incorporate climate projections into planning and design.
- The report concludes that better guidance, updated criteria, and more consistent use of climate projections would help reduce risk to facilities and budgets.

## Action Items

- Secretary of the Army: Update UFC installation master planning standards to require installation-specific risk assessment and response plans.
- Secretary of the Navy: Update UFC installation master planning standards to require installation-specific risk assessment and response plans.
- Secretary of the Air Force: Update UFC installation master planning standards to require installation-specific risk assessment and response plans.
- Secretary of Defense: Issue guidance on incorporating climate projections into installation master planning.
- Secretary of Defense: Issue guidance on incorporating climate projections into facilities project designs.
- Secretary of the Army: Update relevant UFCs to require consideration of climate projections in facilities project designs and incorporate DOD guidance.
- Secretary of the Navy: Update relevant UFCs to require consideration of climate projections in facilities project designs and incorporate DOD guidance.
- Secretary of the Air Force: Update relevant UFCs to require consideration of climate projections in facilities project designs and incorporate DOD guidance.
- DOD: Concurred with all eight recommendations and identified actions to address two of them.
