## Climate Change Risks to DOD Installations

- Climate change and extreme weather events are expected to increase in frequency and severity.
- Documented impacts include loss of life, higher food and energy prices, increased disaster relief and insurance costs, property value fluctuations, and national security concerns.
- DOD facilities face operational and budgetary risks from flooding, sea level rise, drought, wind, wildfire, erosion, and permafrost thaw.

## Climate Information Needed for Planning and Design

- Decision makers need three types of climate information: observed climate conditions, observed climate impacts and vulnerabilities, and future local climate projections.
- Climate information must be tailored to the needs of the user, such as engineers, planners, or federal program managers.
- Climate projections are typically provided as multiple future scenarios over time horizons such as 20, 40, or 60 years, or over a project’s service life.

## DOD Master Planning and Design Standards

- All DOD installations are required to have master plans covering at least 10 years and updated every 5 years.
- Individual facilities projects must follow DOD’s Unified Facilities Criteria (UFC) design standards.
- UFC guidance mentions climate-related factors such as drought, flood, wind, wildfire, rainfall patterns, storm intensity, and water levels.
- Earlier guidance did not clearly specify how to use climate projections in master planning or facility design.

## Installation Examples of Climate Adaptation

- Langley Air Force Base studied sea level rise and raised the minimum elevation for new development.
- Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson developed predictive models for coastal erosion affecting radar sites.
- Fort Irwin developed stormwater drainage improvements after severe flash flooding.
- Marine Corps Recruit Depot Parris Island planned a sea level rise study for use in its next master plan update.
- Naval Station Norfolk identified sea level rise as a risk and set a goal of reducing related impacts in its master plan.
- Naval Base San Diego planned a study of installation-specific sea level rise effects.
- Camp Lejeune used sea level rise study results to inform flood zone mapping and building siting decisions.

## Installations Without Integrated Climate Considerations

- Joint Base Pearl Harbor-Hickam did not consider climate effects in its most recent master plan.
- Pearl Harbor Naval Shipyard also did not integrate climate effects into its most recent master plan.
- Fort Wainwright had not considered climate change in master planning, despite permafrost-related risks in some areas.
- Camp Pendleton was still planning based on historical conditions rather than future climate conditions.

## GAO Conclusions and Prior Findings

- DOD has taken some positive steps, including providing a sea level change scenarios database for DOD sites.
- GAO found that installations have not consistently integrated climate risks into master plans or related planning documents.
- GAO previously reported that DOD had not fully implemented recommendations to clarify climate-related planning actions and update design standards with climate projection guidance.

## Action Items

- DOD: Implement GAO’s recommendations to integrate climate risk assessments into master plans and facility design guidance.
- DOD: Provide clearer guidance on how to use climate projections in master planning and design.
- DOD: Update Unified Facilities Criteria to require consideration of climate projections and define acceptable sources, scenarios, and timeframes.
