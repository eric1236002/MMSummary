## PMIAA Implementation Status

- The meeting reviewed the implementation status of the Program Management Improvement and Accountability Act (PMIAA) across OMB, OPM, the PMPC, and federal agencies.
- OMB used existing GPRAMA processes, strategic reviews, and cross-agency priority goals to support PMIAA implementation.
- OMB developed a five-year PMIAA strategic plan with three strategies: coordinated governance, regular engagement reviews, and strengthening program management capacity.
- OMB issued guidance and took implementation steps, but several PMIAA requirements remained incomplete.

## Program and Project Management Standards

- OMB published 15 program and project management standards in June 2018.
- The standards were described as principle-based and flexible, but less detailed than PMI standards.
- The standards do not include clear minimum thresholds, program-versus-project distinctions, lifecycle guidance, or defined compliance criteria.
- OMB has not yet established a complete governance structure for maintaining, updating, approving, or enforcing the standards.
- Key governance gaps include unclear roles and responsibilities, limited stakeholder engagement, and no formal monitoring or enforcement process.

## PMIAA Portfolio Reviews and High-Risk Areas

- OMB used a pilot review focused on non-IT major acquisition programs to prepare agencies for PMIAA portfolio reviews.
- The pilot helped agencies assess cost, schedule, and performance data and highlighted differences in agency maturity and data quality.
- OMB completed FY 2019 portfolio reviews for ten agencies by October 2019 and held preparatory meetings with 12 agencies.
- OMB planned to expand portfolio reviews to grants in 2020, but had not identified additional program areas.
- OMB used strategic reviews and trilateral meetings to address some high-risk areas, but only five of 35 high-risk areas had trilateral meetings between March 2018 and October 2019.
- The PMPC did not include the High-Risk List on its agendas and was used mainly for sharing program management best practices.

## Federal Program Inventory and Program Definitions

- Agencies continue to have difficulty defining programs consistently for the federal program inventory.
- OMB had not fully acted on prior recommendations to improve the inventory.
- OMB issued a timetable in its June 2019 A-11 guidance, but not full milestones.
- OMB uses different definitions of “program” across PMIAA, GPRAMA, and the DATA Act.
- These definitions were developed independently and have not been reconciled.
- Agencies reported confusion and potential reporting burden caused by the differing definitions.

## OPM Workforce and Career Path Actions

- OPM developed competencies for program and project management, including general and technical competencies.
- OPM updated the 0340 program management job series and issued guidance for identifying project management positions.
- OPM developed a career path for program and project managers and a job identifier code for program and project managers across occupational series.
- OPM planned to release the career path for agency comment by the end of calendar year 2019 and complete the job identifier in 2020.
- Agencies are expected to use these tools for workforce planning, staffing identification, and competency gap analysis.

## Agency Workforce Challenges

- Agencies reported varying ability to identify program and project managers.
- NASA reported it could identify almost all such staff.
- Treasury, Commerce, DOE, and Veterans Affairs reported challenges, especially for staff outside the 0340 series.
- Commerce said it could not accurately identify staff until the OPM job identifier becomes available.

## PMPC Activities and Agency Planning

- OMB established the PMPC and met the statutory minimum for meetings.
- PMPC meetings covered PMIAA implementation updates, OPM workforce progress, and agency best practices.
- Future PMPC meetings were expected to support strategic reviews, review outcomes, update standards, revise strategies, and address high-risk areas.
- Agencies submitted PMIAA implementation plans, but many plans only partially met OMB’s required elements.

## PMIAA Implementation Delays and Measurement

- OMB issued PMIAA implementation guidance in June 2018, six months after the statutory deadline.
- OPM also missed deadlines, releasing competency and classification guidance in 2019 after delays related to internal review and the partial government shutdown.
- Agencies did not report direct impacts from these delays, though one agency said its progress depended on OPM completing the job identifier.
- Agencies proposed different ways to measure PMIAA implementation, including milestones, surveys, maturity models, and existing metrics.
- OMB had not defined specific outcome measures, stating implementation was still early.

## Action Items

- OMB: Continue implementing PMIAA requirements through the strategic plan and agency engagement processes.
- OMB: Further develop governance for program management standards, including roles, stakeholder input, approval, and monitoring.
- OMB: Provide clearer communication to agencies on future portfolio review areas, procedures, and expectations.
- OMB: Continue work on the federal program inventory and provide clearer implementation guidance and milestones.
- OMB: Clarify or reconcile the definitions of “program” across PMIAA, GPRAMA, and the DATA Act.
- OMB: Establish measures to assess PMIAA outcomes.
- OMB: Continue trilateral meetings for high-risk areas and include high-risk discussions in PMPC agendas.
- OPM: Release the program and project manager career path for agency comment by end of calendar year 2019.
- OPM: Complete the job identifier project in 2020.
- Agencies: Continue using PMIAA tools for workforce planning, program portfolio reviews, and tracking cost, schedule, and performance data.
