## IWTU Reengineering Project Status

- EM has been working to reengineer the Integrated Waste Treatment Unit (IWTU) to treat sodium-bearing waste and calcine waste.
- Construction was declared complete in 2012, but the facility required additional repairs and reengineering after testing identified problems.
- As of early 2019, EM was in phase two of a four-phase reengineering approach.
- Phase one focused on identifying equipment and process fixes; phase two focused on implementing those fixes.
- By February 2019, spending on phases one and two was about $150 million, which was approximately $64 million above the original estimate for those phases.
- The project was more than one year behind schedule.
- EM officials indicated phase three could begin in summer 2019 and phase four could begin in early 2020.

## Project Management, Cost, and Schedule Findings

- EM had not developed a full cost estimate for the entire reengineering effort at the time of review.
- The cost estimate for phases one and two partially met best practices for being comprehensive.
- The project schedule partially met best practices for being well-constructed and substantially met best practices for being comprehensive.
- The schedule included long-duration critical path activities and large amounts of positive and negative float.
- EM and DOE have not fully defined requirements for classifying work as operations activity versus capital asset project.
- The IWTU reengineering effort was managed as an operations activity after construction ended in 2012.
- Operations activities are subject to less stringent requirements than capital asset projects.

## Earned Value Management (EVM)

- EM generally met best practices for maintaining an EVM system and using EVM data for decision-making.
- The EVM data were considered unreliable due to anomalies in every month reviewed, including missing or negative values.
- EM did not document the reasons for the anomalies in monthly reports.
- Unreliable EVM data may limit EM’s ability to measure project performance and support informed decisions.

## DOE Oversight and Operational Release Requirements

- DOE established an operational release milestone requirement for selected projects with extended transitions to operations.
- Program offices must prepare an operational release plan and provide quarterly progress updates to DOE’s Project Management Risk Committee (PMRC).
- EM developed an operational release plan for IWTU in December 2016.
- EM provided five progress updates to the PMRC, but not on a quarterly basis.
- The PMRC made recommendations in three of the five briefings.

## Sodium-Bearing Waste (SBW) Disposal Challenges

- EM does not have a strategy or timeline for disposing of SBW or identifying an alternative disposal path.
- EM’s preferred disposal site has been the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP).
- Major barriers include the WIPP permit, federal restrictions on HLW disposal, and WIPP capacity limitations.
- EM published a Federal Register notice in October 2018 and a supplemental notice in June 2019 seeking comment on a proposed interpretation of the statutory definition of HLW.
- Stakeholder comments were mixed, with some supporting the approach and others raising legal and procedural concerns.
- EM stated it had not set a timeline for implementation or specified the administrative actions needed.
- If WIPP disposal is not possible, EM’s fallback is disposal in a geologic repository after treatment, although no such repository currently exists in the United States.

## Calcine Waste Treatment and Disposal

- EM suspended further development of hot isostatic pressing for calcine waste due to technical challenges and uncertainty about the disposal path.
- Hot isostatic pressing had been selected in 2009, but later reviews raised concerns about maturity, logistics, safety, and cost.
- EM has not developed a strategy or timeline for final calcine treatment and disposal.
- EM is evaluating alternatives, including direct disposal and retrieval of waste from storage bin sets.
- A pilot retrieval project for the oldest bin set was initiated to test retrieval methods and transfer waste to a partially empty bin set.
- The pilot project was estimated to cost $50 million over five years.

## Regulatory and Legal Framework

- Mixed HLW and SBW at INL are governed by federal statutes, DOE orders, EPA hazardous waste requirements, and state-authorized hazardous waste programs.
- DOE regulates radioactive waste under the Atomic Energy Act and the Nuclear Waste Policy Act.
- Hazardous waste components are regulated under RCRA by EPA or authorized state programs.
- Idaho is authorized to administer its own hazardous waste program.
- HLW generally must be disposed of in a geologic repository unless NRC approves an alternative.
- Vitrification was identified as the treatment standard for Idaho’s mixed HLW under EPA requirements.
