## Legal and Regulatory Framework for Mixed HLW

- Mixed high-level waste (HLW) at Idaho National Laboratory (INL) is governed by federal laws, cleanup agreements, DOE orders, EPA RCRA regulations, and state-authorized hazardous waste programs.
- DOE defines HLW under the Atomic Energy Act and Nuclear Waste Policy Act; calcine waste is treated by DOE as HLW, and sodium-bearing waste (SBW) is managed by EM as mixed HLW.
- HLW must generally be disposed of in a geologic repository unless the Nuclear Regulatory Commission approves an alternative.
- EPA regulations require hazardous components of mixed HLW to meet treatment standards before land disposal; Idaho’s standard for mixed HLW is vitrification.
- Idaho and New Mexico both operate EPA-authorized hazardous waste programs; New Mexico’s program includes permitting for WIPP.

## IWTU Reengineering Project Status

- As of March 2019, EM’s Integrated Waste Treatment Unit (IWTU) reengineering project was in phase two of a four-phase approach to achieve operational capability.
- Phase one focused on identifying fixes through engineering analyses and chemistry studies.
- Phase two focused on implementing fixes, including equipment modifications.
- EM officials reported that phases one and two had cost about $150 million by February 2019, which was about $64 million over the original estimate for those phases combined.
- The project was more than one year behind schedule.
- EM expected phase three to begin in summer 2019 and phase four to begin in early 2020, but key outage and testing decisions had not yet been finalized.
- Treatment of all 900,000 gallons of SBW was originally estimated to take 10 months, but EM now estimated it could take 3 to 7 years.

## Project Management Requirements and Oversight

- DOE Order 413.3B governs capital asset projects, while EM manages operations activities under a separate cleanup policy issued in July 2017.
- In a prior review, GAO found that EM and DOE had not clearly established criteria for classifying work as either an operations activity or a capital asset project.
- EM has managed the IWTU reengineering project as an operations activity since construction was declared complete in April 2012.
- DOE created the operational release milestone in 2016 to provide additional oversight for projects transitioning from construction to operations.
- Under this process, program offices must develop an operational release plan and provide quarterly progress updates to the Project Management Risk Committee (PMRC), unless an alternative schedule is approved.
- EM developed an operational release plan in December 2016 and provided five progress briefings to the PMRC, though not on a quarterly basis.

## Cost and Schedule Estimate Findings

- EM partially met best practices for a comprehensive cost estimate.
- The cost estimate did not use a standardized work breakdown structure and did not account for all life-cycle costs, including costs for later phases that had not yet been developed at the time of review.
- EM partially met best practices for a well-constructed schedule.
- The schedule had minimal sequencing issues and a continuous critical path, but included long-duration critical path activities and unusually large positive and negative float values.
- EM substantially met best practices for a comprehensive schedule, including capturing most activities and assigning resources to most work.
- EM has not developed a total project cost estimate or completion date covering the full IWTU reengineering effort and future waste treatment operations.

## Earned Value Management (EVM) Findings

- EM generally met best practices for using an EVM system, including having a comprehensive system and using EVM data for decision-making.
- EM partially met best practices for ensuring EVM data reliability.
- The reviewed EVM data contained anomalies in all months examined, including missing and negative values.
- EM did not document all anomalies and their explanations in monthly reports.
- EM officials attributed many anomalies to authorized unpriced work in phase two.
- The report found that unreliable EVM data may limit EM’s ability to measure project performance.

## Sodium-Bearing Waste (SBW) Disposal Challenges

- EM identified WIPP as its preferred disposal site for SBW, but no final disposal decision had been made as of March 2019.
- EM faces three main obstacles: WIPP permit restrictions, the federal prohibition on HLW disposal at WIPP, and WIPP capacity limitations.
- New Mexico’s WIPP permit prohibits waste that has ever been managed as HLW from being disposed of at WIPP unless the permit is modified.
- DOE has pursued a new interpretation of the statutory definition of HLW through Federal Register notices in 2018 and 2019.
- Stakeholder comments on the proposed interpretation included both support and concern, including concerns about excluding NRC from the determination process.
- EM officials stated there was no timeline for implementing the new HLW interpretation or for making related policy or regulatory changes.
- EM also has not developed a strategy or timeline for its preferred SBW disposal path or an alternative path.

## Calcine Waste Treatment and Disposal Challenges

- EM suspended further development of its calcine waste treatment plan for land disposal due to challenges with the chosen technology and uncertainty about disposal pathways.
- EM had selected hot isostatic pressing in 2009, but later analyses identified major technical, regulatory, safety, and cost concerns.
- An April 2016 analysis of alternatives recommended deferring a final treatment decision until disposal-path performance requirements are better defined.
- EM has no strategy or timeline for selecting the next treatment approach for calcine waste.
- EM is conducting a pilot retrieval project to remove calcine waste from the oldest bin set and transfer it to a partially empty bin set.
- The retrieval project is intended to test technologies and reduce use of the older storage bin set.

## GAO Recommendations and DOE Response

- GAO made five recommendations to DOE.
- DOE agreed with recommendations related to IWTU cost estimates, schedule estimates, EVM reliability, and strategies for SBW and calcine waste disposal.
- DOE stated that phases three and four cost estimates and schedules had been developed and reviewed, and that EVM data would be incorporated once contract negotiations were completed.
- DOE disagreed with the recommendation to seek congressional clarification on DOE’s authority to classify SBW as other than HLW; GAO deleted that recommendation but maintained that litigation risk remains.

## Action Items

- Secretary of Energy / Assistant Secretary for Environmental Management: Develop comprehensive cost estimates for the IWTU reengineering project and SBW treatment operations.
- Secretary of Energy / Assistant Secretary for Environmental Management: Develop well-constructed schedule estimates for the IWTU reengineering project and SBW treatment operations.
- Secretary of Energy / Assistant Secretary for Environmental Management: Follow best practices to ensure reliability of the IWTU reengineering project’s EVM system.
- Secretary of Energy / Assistant Secretary for Environmental Management: Develop a strategy, including a timeline, for implementing the preferred or an alternative SBW disposal pathway.
- Secretary of Energy / Assistant Secretary for Environmental Management: Develop a strategy, including a timeline, to identify and develop a treatment approach for calcine waste by December 31, 2035.
