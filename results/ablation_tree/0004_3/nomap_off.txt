## Overview

- The transcript summarizes a GAO review of how federal, state, and local agencies investigate, report, and enforce cases of abuse in U.S. nursing homes.
- It focuses on CMS oversight of nursing home compliance with Medicare and Medicaid participation requirements, including resident rights, quality of care, and quality of life.

## Oversight Structure

- CMS oversees nursing homes at the federal level through:
  - The central office, which sets standards and guidance
  - Regional offices, which oversee state-level implementation
  - State survey agencies, which conduct inspections, handle complaints, and investigate reported incidents
- When violations are identified, possible responses include:
  - Corrective plans
  - Re-surveys
  - Civil money penalties
  - Payment denials
  - Termination from Medicare and Medicaid participation

## Abuse Investigation and Reporting Process

- Nursing homes must immediately report suspected abuse to the state survey agency.
- Nursing homes must investigate suspected abuse internally.
- Nursing homes must submit a written report within 5 working days.
- If abuse is confirmed, the state survey agency must refer the case to:
  - Local law enforcement
  - Medicaid Fraud Control Units, when applicable
  - Nurse aide registries or relevant licensing authorities
- If a nurse aide is found to have committed abuse, the aide is placed on the state registry and the nursing home may not rehire the aide.

## Other Involved Entities

- Adult Protective Services
- Long-term care ombudsmen
- Local police
- Medicaid Fraud Control Units

## GAO Findings on Abuse Violations

- Abuse-related deficiencies increased from 430 in 2013 to 875 in 2017.
- The share of severe cases also increased.
- About two-thirds of abuse violations were identified through complaint investigations or facility-reported incident investigations.
- CMS enforcement actions for abuse violations were often not carried out; when imposed, civil money penalties were the most common.
- Many unenforced penalties were not imposed because facilities corrected the issues before the effective date.
- Some nursing homes were cited for abuse violations in multiple years, suggesting recurring problems.

## Common Abuse Types and Perpetrators

- Common abuse types included:
  - Physical abuse
  - Verbal or psychological abuse
  - Sexual abuse
- In the GAO sample:
  - Staff were the most common perpetrators
  - Residents were the next most common
  - Family members and visitors were cited in fewer cases

## Risk Factors and Investigation Challenges

- Resident-related risk factors included:
  - Few visitors
  - Cognitive impairment
  - Mixed age groups living together
- Facility and staffing-related risk factors included:
  - Staffing shortages
  - Inadequate staff training
  - Incomplete background checks
- Investigation challenges included:
  - Underreporting
  - Difficulty obtaining testimony from residents with cognitive impairment
  - Limited facility cooperation
  - Poor coordination among agencies

## CMS System Gaps Identified by GAO

- CMS databases did not clearly record the type of abuse or the type of perpetrator.
- CMS did not provide enough guidance on what information facilities should include in incident reports.
- Referral processes had delays and missed referrals.
- The definition of substantiated abuse was not sufficiently clear.
- Rules for sharing information with law enforcement were not clear.

## GAO Recommendations and HHS Response

- GAO recommended that CMS:
  - Require reporting of abuse type and perpetrator type in data systems
  - Create standardized reporting forms and guidance
  - Refer cases to law enforcement immediately when there is reasonable suspicion
  - Strengthen oversight of state agency referrals
  - Clarify that substantiated abuse must be reported even if no deficiency is cited
  - Specify what information should be shared with law enforcement
- HHS agreed with all six recommendations and said it was taking steps to improve data collection, reporting templates, timely referrals, and standardization of information.

## Action Items

- CMS: Require reporting of abuse type and perpetrator type in abuse-related data
- CMS: Develop standardized incident reporting forms and guidance
- CMS: Refer cases to law enforcement immediately when there is reasonable suspicion of abuse
- CMS: Strengthen oversight of state agency referral practices
- CMS: Clarify reporting requirements for substantiated abuse
- CMS: Specify required information to share with law enforcement
- HHS: Implement measures to improve data collection, reporting templates, timely referrals, and information standardization
